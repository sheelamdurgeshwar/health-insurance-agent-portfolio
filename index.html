HAVE QUESTIONS?

Frequently Asked Questions

Get clear, compliance-friendly answers to common concerns about coverage periods, network rules, and federal marketplace subsidies.

For standard Affordable Care Act (ACA) policies, the Open Enrollment Period typically runs from November 1 to January 15 each year. Submitting enrollments before December 15 ensures a January 1 coverage start date. Outside these dates, you can only enroll if you trigger a Qualifying Life Event (e.g., marriage, birth of a child, relocation, or loss of job-based insurance), which opens a 60-day Special Enrollment Period (SEP).

Whether you keep your doctor depends on the network type of your new plan. PPO (Preferred Provider Organization) plans allow you to see out-of-network doctors, though in-network care is cheaper. HMO (Health Maintenance Organization) plans restrict coverage strictly to in-network providers except in emergencies. As your agent, I perform a direct network formulary lookup using your doctor's name to confirm their network inclusions before writing a policy.

Government subsidies are based on your Modified Adjusted Gross Income (MAGI) relative to the Federal Poverty Level. Under the ACA, you can receive Premium Tax Credits which lower your monthly cost, and if eligible, Cost-Sharing Reductions (CSR) which lower your out-of-pocket deductibles and copays on Silver-tier plans. I analyze these limits to maximize your eligible tax credits during filing.

An HMO requires primary doctor referrals for specialists and limits care to a specific network. A PPO gives you freedom to see any doctor without referrals. An HSA-eligible plan is a High-Deductible Health Plan (HDHP) paired with a Health Savings Account, letting you pay for medical costs using tax-free dollars. HSA funds never expire, making them an excellent wealth and health double-benefit.
